Rank-48 and rank-49 circuits
---------------------------
Derived from PLinOpt, by Jean-Guillaume Dumas, Bruno Grenet, Clement Pernet,
and Alexandre Sedoglavic: https://github.com/jgdumas/plinopt
Pinned upstream commit: 1af82ca4df7ed7d9687bc5f9a39bc10e724e1e0f.
These derived circuit portions retain the CeCILL-B Version 1.0 terms;
see LICENSE_CeCILL-B.txt and PLINOPT_AUTHORS.txt. Original source SLPs and
hashes are also retained in the prior implementation/rank48_source and
implementation/winograd49_source directories of the complete bundle.

Stable alternative-basis rank-7 circuits
---------------------------------------
Adapted from Appendix A.1 of "Alternative Basis matrix multiplication is
fast and stable", by Oded Schwartz, Sivan Toledo, Noa Vaknin and Gal Wiernik.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00211-026-01531-9
Article license: CC BY 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/
The adaptation changes signs and absorbs coordinate permutations into
argument/output view tuples. This preserves the exact bilinear identities
and absolute-coefficient error-growth quantities. Author attribution and
the article/license links must accompany redistribution of these portions.

New implementation work
-----------------------
Python translation, generated direct-ufunc/out interfaces, batching engine,
paired-axis traversal, benchmarks, and permutation adaptation were prepared
by ChatGPT on 2026-10-07. The two source licenses above apply to their
respective derived portions; this notice does not assign a single blanket
license to the whole implementation. No warranty is provided.

The counter and benchmarks use public Flopscope 0.12.0+np2.4.6 operations.
No private numerical entry points or unmetered array arithmetic are used
inside multiply(). Ordinary NumPy is used only by separate validation code.

Rank-20 <2,3,4> circuits
------------------------
The rank-20 <2,3,4> scheme is due to Hopcroft and Kerr (1971); its tensor is as
listed in Sedoglavic's catalogue, https://fmm.univ-lille.fr/2x3x4.html. The
54-node straight-line programs derive from Andrew Perminov's
FastMatrixMultiplication repository,
https://github.com/dronperminov/FastMatrixMultiplication
(schemes/results/addition_reduced_ZT/2x3x4_m20_cr54_fv22_cn90_ZT_reduced.json),
MIT License, Copyright (c) 2025 Andrew Perminov; see LICENSE_MIT_Perminov.txt.
The six orientations come from tensor transposition and duality; every circuit
was checked exactly against its tensor before use.

Rank-40 <3,3,6> and <3,6,3> circuits
------------------------------------
The rank-40 circuits for <3,3,6> and its <3,6,3> orientation (the r40 top
levels of the fast-matmul kernel) are also derived from PLinOpt, by
Jean-Guillaume Dumas, Bruno Grenet, Clement Pernet, and Alexandre Sedoglavic:
https://github.com/jgdumas/plinopt. They retain the CeCILL-B Version 1.0 terms
stated above; see LICENSE_CeCILL-B.txt and PLINOPT_AUTHORS.txt. The <3,3,6>
rank-40 tensor is listed in Sedoglavic's catalogue,
https://fmm.univ-lille.fr/3x3x6.html, with its original authors.

Rank-54 <3,4,6> scheme (AlphaEvolve)
------------------------------------
The rank-54 <3,4,6> scheme, used in its <3,4,6>, <3,6,4>, <4,3,6>, <4,6,3> and
<6,4,3> orientations, is due to Alexander Novikov, Ngan Vu, Marvin Eisenberger, Emilien
Dupont, Po-Sen Huang, Adam Zsolt Wagner, Sergey Shirobokov, Borislav Kozlovskii,
Francisco J. R. Ruiz, Abbas Mehrabian, M. Pawan Kumar, Abigail See, Swarat
Chaudhuri, George Holland, Alex Davies, Sebastian Nowozin, Pushmeet Kohli, and
Matej Balog, "AlphaEvolve: A coding agent for scientific and algorithmic
discovery", Google DeepMind technical report, May 2025,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131.
We took its decomposition from Sedoglavic's catalogue,
https://fmm.univ-lille.fr/3x4x6.html. The authors' results repository,
https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphaevolve_results, states: "All software
is licensed under the Apache License, Version 2.0 (Apache 2.0)" and "All other
materials are licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0
International License (CC-BY)". This attribution accompanies the scheme on
redistribution.

Structured triangular and Gram circuits (flip-cpd)
--------------------------------------------------
The structured block-product circuits used by the triangular and Gram
(A A^T) products of the covariance step (the _TR_FACTOR_UG and _TR_FACTOR_GT
graphs, run by _tr_factor_product, _tr_factor_triangular and _tr_factor_gram)
derive from Kirill Khoruzhii, Patrick Gelss, and Sebastian Pokutta, "Faster
Algorithms for Structured Matrix Multiplication via Flip Graph Search",
arXiv:2511.10786 (2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10786, and its released
coefficient code, https://github.com/khoruzhii/flip-cpd (commit
9eeb17f487f14103d7128cf290ae61535d8983b1), MIT License, Copyright (c) 2025
Kirill Khoruzhii; see LICENSE-flip-cpd. Negations were folded into the
consumers' add/sub signs; the bilinear identities are unchanged.

Cumulant propagation (Alignment Research Center reference code)
---------------------------------------------------------------
The third-order cumulant ("K3") propagation at the core of this estimator
started as a port of the Alignment Research Center's reference implementation
of cumulant propagation, https://github.com/alignment-research-center/mlp_cumulant_propagation
(src/mlp_kprop; commit 93d091a4c26c042bfffa28f2e76a81bc0aba94bb), which
accompanies Wilson Wu, Victor Lecomte, Michael Winer, George Robinson, Jacob
Hilton, and Paul Christiano, "Estimating the expected output of wide random
MLPs more efficiently than sampling", arXiv:2605.05179 (2026),
https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05179. MIT License, Copyright (c) 2026 Alignment
Research Center; see LICENSE_MIT_ARC.txt. Our port was rewritten for
flopscope, restructured to fit the FLOP budget, and extended.
